I used two metaphors for yielding — fishing and yoga. Think of a few of your own. What activities do you do that require this approach? Describe the process. Teach it to someone who has never done it.

Yielding is to just give yourself away in something. Its like playing music Maybe a great master improvising. Some great classical pianist like. Maybe a marathon runner. There are so many distracting thoughts. You always tell yourself to give up. You have to drain out those voices. Just give in to your training, and your ability.
SO yeah one metaphor can be running a marathon. The result is too far. There might not be big wins. Your mind will tell you to quit. It will ache. And you will be exhausted. Just give in to your training. Just let your body do it’s job and you will reach the finish line.
The analogy fell flat a little bit. I could’ve probably thought of more similar things. Running a marathon. What else could be yielding. Is it like love? You don’t know what’s happening. But you just keep on doing things because there is a chance that you will win in the end. Fuck. That is so bad. 
Yielding. TO give oneself. It’s like a religious experience. Yield in war. How is it like a religious experience. Spiritual experience. You wait and wait. And do what? Nothing. Like attaining nirvana? No. Attaining nirvana is like the final finish line.
I can compare it to just about anything here.

Yielding is like playing cricket. You play according to the conditions. You defend. You leave. You try everything in your power to stay on the pitch. Then after a while, you start seeing the ball in slow motion. It’s as if you already knew where the bowler will pitch the ball. This is the same ball that you’ve been defending and leaving, and somehow trying to keep out of your stumps. You hit this one to the boundary in one swift motion. And you will get those times more you stay on the pitch. You will defend. You will leave. And you will take it on your body. But if you stick around, you will reach the century.

Yielding is like being in a relationship. You will have arguments. You will have fights. That’s just a part of it.
