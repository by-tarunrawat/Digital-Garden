Date: 24-12-2018
Topic: Write about a wild-eyed dream.

When I was little, I dreamed about playing cricket literally every night. I wanted to become a cricketer. Play for India. Play seriously. I just wanted to play for a living, not doing anything else. I remember, I used to dream I am on the pitch with Sachin Tendulkar, some nights I would win games, make centuries. I would wake up with a jerk if I was not playing well or something — practicing the shot after waking up.
My Grandmother was a big cricket fan. She would stay up at night to watch cricket. Test matches, one day matches. She was crazy about it. A small woman, old and wrinkled, doesn’t understand English. I would have proper talks with my grandmother. It never felt unusual to me. I don’t know, I thought it was a normal thing. Now that I’m writing about it, it feels really unusual. She would wake up at 4.30-5 to watch a test match going on in Australia. Would follow every ball of a match. She would certainly have insightful things to say about the match. 
My grandmother started me in cricket. She would bowl to me while I batted. She thought I’d become a big cricketer. Sometimes I wonder if I could have. I feel like if I had focused on it, I could’ve played on some level. That also sounds like an excuse. I could’ve but I didn’t. Because? If I didn’t really do it, then it doesn’t matter if I could have or not. It’s all in the air. I can say whatever I want, there is no way to prove it. It’s all conjecture.
I just haven’t done enough for something that I loved so much. Now that I think about it, it was all a lack of effort. There is a lot of ignorance in there as well. Maybe I didn’t love it enough. I was stupid for sure - didn’t know shit. 
Am I assigning blame? Am I criticizing? I don’t know. I’ve learnt one thing though. If you really love something you need to give it your all. It is a very difficult rule to follow. But it feels true. I won’t pretend to know. But thinking about things that could’ve been, I feel going all out was something I could’ve done more in life. It is difficult. There are so may distractions. There is shame, embarrassment, fear. You have to think about failure. What if you fail? What if you don’t know enough? What if you’re wrong? 
As a kid, I wish someone told me there are easy answers to these questions. When I was a kid, I was too shy to walk into a cricket academy. I wish there was someone who told me, it doesn’t matter. You’re not shy. You’re just afraid. And you know, everyone in the world is afraid. Everyone is afraid of doing things. You shouldn’t be. There is one thing I want you to learn today. Do things. Don’t hold yourself from doing things you want to do. It’s a recipe for an unsatisfactory future. 
I was taught the opposite lesson. Don’t be yourself. Don’t say anything. DO things this way. Think about what other people will think. Think about what society will say. I’m still told these things at home. My uncle told me to change myself during interviews. Which might be a good advice to get through a job interview, but a terrible advice to get through life. Pretend. Pretend you don’t when you do or do when you don’t. Pretend to be calm when you are simmering inside. Pretend wrong is right. Pretend they know better. Pretend you know better. Just pretend, pretend, pretend.
