
At Boeing, I did **Requirement Analysis** based on what the client wants. In the writer's room, I do **Motivation Analysis** based on what the characters want. If a character’s 'Requirement' (their goal) isn't met, the story is 'broken code'—it doesn't move ahead. 

When I wrote the **Rakshabandhan finale**, I was basically doing a **Regression Test** on the previous episodes. I had to make sure the ending 'compiled' with every bit of logic we set up months ago. That 70% rating spike? That was just a successful 'System Deployment' without bugs.


The research agency gave me **User Feedback** on a show called **Dear Ishq**. Based on the feedback, a change was needed in the show. Instead of a UI change and functional change, I did a **Genre Change**. I re-architected the 'Story Engine' from a romantic 'Frontend' to a thriller 'Backend' to increase 'User Engagement' (viewership) by 50%.

For the Boeing project that had been in deployment for several years, I had to quickly come in, learn and start taking it forward. Similarly, for shows like **Bhabhiji ghar me hain**, Happu ki ultan paltan, Rakshabandhan and many others, that had been running for years, I had to quickly come in, learn and take the story forward.
