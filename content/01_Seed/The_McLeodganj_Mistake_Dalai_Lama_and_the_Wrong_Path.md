Date: 15-04-2019
Topic: Write about a town you passed through

It was a few years ago. 7 years to be exact. My first year in MICA had just finished. First years had to do a rural project after the year had ended. We had to as well. We decided to do it in Dehradun. I would get to go home. And my friends would get to see my city. Our plan was to finish up our project in Dehradun, and go to some hill station for the remaining days. We decided on Mcleodganj.
Mcleodganj is a tiny hill town in Himachal. Majestic mountains on all sides. Gorgeous scenery. Fresh air and lovely people. I went there with 7 of my friends for a couple of days. One of the most memorable trips I’ve been on. 
The town was filled with Tibetians. It is like a sanctuary for them. Safe from the Chinese government. The day I reached, we found out that Dalai Lama was also there. He was scheduled to speak at a Buddhist monastery. I went there to see him. I’m not a religious person. Nor am I big on religious leaders. But there is something about this guy. Someone who preaches peace and non violence. Loving fellow humans. Loving all living things. I hadn’t seen many famous people before. And this one was one of the world’s most famous. 
The guest house we lived in was a big house. Compared to the other buildings in the town at least. We had to go down a hill to reach it. Which was easy. It was the climbing up that would get us out of breath. But the scenery everywhere was just breathtaking. We had to go way down to reach our guest house. But the path went much lower than that. We could see little houses under the mountains. Trees everywhere. Uneven playing grounds with lush grass.
On the second day we went for a trek. It was a thing recommended for tourists. The weather was nice and chilly. So I didn’t think it was going to be too difficult. Growing up in the mountains, it wasn’t for me. Some of my friends struggled. It was probably their first time on mountains of this majesty. One of them lost their shit on the way. She breathed heavily. Got angry at nothing. And threw a fit. Everyone had some trouble getting up. 
Once we reached the peak, everyone forgot their problems. It was beautiful. Mountains and trees and huge rocks as far as we could see. By the time we reached, clouds had gathered. Some of them were at our level. It was amazing. While starting our trek, someone had suggested to camp there for the night. We decided not to. Big mistake. I would have loved to have seen the stars at night. The cold night air and the darkness would have been terrifying. I would have loved it. As it turns out, we had to go back. We couldn’t rent tents up there. 
Going back, we took another path. It lead to our destination as well. But it wasn’t the one we had come up on. We lost one of our friends. We shouted her name. Tried calling. But we didn’t have signals on our phones. A friend and I ran back up and down to look for her. We completed the trek that took us more than two hours one way, in fifteen minutes looking for her. Finally we found her at the base. She had taken the correct path. We took the wrong one. 
As soon as she saw us, tears rolled down her eyes. I took her in my arms immediately. I was relieved. I felt guilty over losing her. We were happy to see each other. Later that night, all of us had a good laugh over it.
There are many reasons why I still remember that trip. The mountains, beautiful sceneries, the weather, the company. I sometimes wish I could live there for a few months. Maybe I will someday. I hope it remains the same whenever I do. Maybe I’m hoping for too much.
