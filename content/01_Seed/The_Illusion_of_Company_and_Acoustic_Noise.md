Date: 20-12-2018
Topic: "Everybody loves the sound of a train in the distance. Everybody thinks it's true." (after Paul Simon)

There is a smidgen of truth to that. In the city it is just another sound. Adding to the crowd of noises that is always there — standing on the fence, making noise, but after a while ignored and made part of the background. There is no love in that. There is a longing in love. I don’t want the train to come. I don’t want to hear the loud victory march of the train driver over another human settlement. Along with the car horns and street vendors and running engines. Like an accompaniments of an orchestra that doesn’t go with each other. Out of rhythm and playing different tunes.
It is not subtle. But it’s omnipresence has made us almost unaware of it. We don’t know life without it. It is the sound of the universe now. Always there. Like the stars and galaxies. Like the proportion of elements in the air we breathe. The same oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide. We just assume its naturalness. 

The truth in it is the obvious scene. We imagine quite and peace. Tucked in bed. About to fall asleep. We are thinking about our day, our life. The conversations we had, the people we met. Things that you wish you could’ve said. Things you wish you didn’t. Old loves, new crushes. Your work, your friends and your plans for the future. And then suddenly in the distance the train hoots. Your mind comes back to the present. This is where you are, this is where you should be. In the here and now. You hear the track in rhythm with the train. It’s lovely music. The train blows it’s trumpet once again and it goes beautifully with the rhythm. The silence plays a part. It provides the perfect background for this concert to work.
We don’t value silence anymore. We want to fill it up with something. There is an addiction to sound. We hear it everywhere in the background. We have built an immunity to it. When we are alone with our thoughts, we look for noise. In our phones. On the TV in the background while you’re working on something on your laptop. We think that noise gives us company. We are creating an illusion of company with the noises that we can do without.s
