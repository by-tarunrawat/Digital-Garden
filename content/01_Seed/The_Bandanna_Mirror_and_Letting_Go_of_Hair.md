Topic: Write about hair

I used to spend hours everyday on may hair. Looking good meant good clothes and a trendy hairstyle. I always wanted to grow my hair long. I succeeded once. If you took a group of hair from front and measure it across the length of the face, my hair came under my chin. This was the most I had grown my hair. Unfortunately this was at a time when I started losing my hair. This endeavor couldn’t last very long.
I had great hair. That’s what I felt at least. I often felt that the barber should pay me to come for a haircut. I was giving away my silky, luscious hair for free. He could weave my hair into glorious wigs and earn thousands.
I started losing hair around the time I grew my hair long. There was a bald patch developing in the middle. You could see my round skull if there was light directly above my head. The scarce growth got invisible in the presence of intense light. I would comb my hair in a certain way. Left to right. Covering up the slowly growing bald spot. It would take time. Someday I would get it right in 5 minutes. Sometimes it would take 15.
I went to a few doctors. Dermatologists. They gave me medicines to eat. A liquid to put on my head. This worked actually. I could see a little growth of hair. Very little. But it was there. It roughened the rest of my hair.  I stopped the other medicines. But I stuck to the lotion. It was working.
I had an entire ritual to put it on. I would put it at night. Apply the required amount in the balding area. Spread it. Then comb my hair. But on a bandanna across my forehead. So that the lotion doesn’t drip on my face. I would sleep like this. Then I would get up in the morning, put the lotion again. 
I was so attached to my hair. I couldn’t think about a life without them. And as much as I was attached to them, I was embarrassed about getting bald. Once semester, I wore a baseball hat to college everyday. 3rd semester I think. Nobody had seen my head for 6 months. Not even my best friend. I would wear it under the helmet even. I would tell people it was because I was getting some medicines for an infection on my head. It was true. Partly.
Last two years of college, I took off the hat. I was still struggling with hair loss. But it wasn’t as severe for now. The medicine was doing more or less doing its job. It was after college that my hair started a mass exodus. My whole head was covered with fewer hair. My mom was worried. How would I ever find a girl to marry if I’m bald. 
I had been thinking about shaving my head for a while. But I didn’t have the courage to do it. After a lot of months struggling with the decision, I decided to go for it. I was nervous. I had never done this before. Somebody had told me that hair grow better if you shave them off once. So, there was still some hope. But there was also the possibility of never seeing my hair again.
The barber was surprised by my request. It’s inauspicious to shave your hair. You do it when people in your family die. Most bald people live with the two patches of hair on either side of the head, connected with a growth at the back. With nothing in the middle. Some people grow one side of the patch. And comb it across their bald head to the other side. Do they think they can fool people into thinking they have a ‘bitchin’ hairstyle. It looks ridiculous and you have a better chance of convincing people that the earth is flat. My Nani wanted me to do it. I refused. 
I was still nervous as I came out of the barber shop. I had seen myself in the mirror inside. It didn’t look bad. I didn’t look horrible. I could feel the air on my head. It was pleasant. As I was walking back home. I saw people looking at me. Some were blatant in their observations, but most used their peripheral vision. It didn’t really bother me for some reason. For some unknown reason I was smiling. I was happy. 
My mom laughed at my face. It didn’t bother me. I sent a picture to my friend. He came running. He loved it. It didn’t effect me. I wasn’t spending hours in front of the mirror. Now that my hair were gone, I didn’t care about what other people thought about it. I felt free. 
I didn’t really know how it would affect the rest of my life. Would I still be attractive to women? Would I look funny? Will people make fun of me? I didn’t give a fuck. I had embraced my baldness. I had no hope of getting them back. And I was fine with it. I wish I had known I had to let them go to be truly happy about them.
