Topic: Write about memories underfoot.

I don’t know what it means. Maybe it means memories I have on my feet. Maybe it means travel. Yeah that seems much more like it. 
I haven’t traveled a lot, I confess. Not as much as I want to or as much as I could. I’ve let go of several opportunities to travel. I have yet to go outside my own country. Technically I have. But I was so young that I don’t remember any of it.
As kids the only places we had seen was Dehradun — where I grew up, Kotdwara — where my dad grew up, and Uttarkashi — where my mom grew up. We didn’t travel anywhere at all. That wasn’t something our family did. Travel for fun. Just because we haven’t seen the place. Nothing like that. 
It is funny because my dad traveled a lot for his job. He was in the merchant navy. So he had so many miles under his feet that I would have travel everyday for the next 50 years to be able to reach his level. When my dad was home, he didn’t like to go anywhere. So we didn’t make any plans. I didn’t know families and people traveled for fun. It’s only recently that I found out from my friends that they used to travel together to new places as a family. It was a surprise to me.
The first time I had gone out of home was when I was 23. I was starting a new job and had to go to Mysore. My dad and mom had come with me to see me off into a new life. It was a new life for me. Living on my own. Being responsible for your own survival. Food, cleaning, washing, there was no one else to take care of that for me. I really enjoyed it.
Since then I’ve put in some miles under my feet. I went to Hyderabad for a couple of months after that. Then Pune for two years, Ahmedabad for four and now Bangalore for another four. That makes it 10 years. Wow. It’s been a while. In between those ten years, I’ve managed to see other cities, towns and villages in India. 
I stayed in Mumbai for two months. I hated it living there. The time I was there I was cramped for room. Everywhere there are small spaces and too many people to fill them up. I remember traveling in the local everyday. My nose in someone’s armpit. Pubs were always crowded on weekends. I had to maneuver my hand through the crowd of hips, backs and shoulders to bring my beer to my lips. It isn’t a fun weekend in Bombay if you haven’t spilled beer on your shirt. Spilling beer also comes as a relief. You are so drenched in sweat that any other liquid hardly makes a difference. 
All that was still manageable. I’ve lived in hostel with other boys. So, I can deal with crowded spaces. But the thing I hated the most was the weather. I’ve lived close to mountains all my life. I’m used to colder weather. Even in summers, if it was hot, it would be dry. Bombay was humid and hot. Like any coastal city. I would leave for the office in the morning and by the time I reached, My shirt would be completely drenched. I had to start wearing a vest inside. It helped with the wet shirt, but it did not help with the heat. 
While living in Pune I saw Lonavla, Khandala, Mahaballeshwar. These were hill stations that people flocked to, to get some reprieve from the city heat and pollution. I thought these places were okay. It was nice and cold and hilly. But I’m used to a much better mountain experience. Deep crevices in between mountains that people come to see, was an everyday thing. 
I did like the strawberries in Mahaballeshwar though. They were sweet and juicy. Just the way I like them. And they were everywhere. 10s of people selling them in carts and baskets. Trees and gardens full of strawberries. It was an amazing sight. I think, more than eating, I loved looking at them. 
I’ve lived in Bangalore for the past 4 years. Although technically I don’t live here anymore. I’ve left my rented apartment. I move around. Sometimes I go to Pune. Stay there for a couple of weeks with friends. Sometimes I come back to Bangalore for a month or two to live with my girlfriend. Otherwise I go back home, to Dehradun. I don’t like living there anymore. My friends have all moved out. There is not much to do. And I can’t drive. So I can’t go around the city. And to think I never wanted to leave that place.
Living in Bangalore has been good. It is comfortable. The weather is nice and I have friends here. I’ve had good and bad memories here. I have made friends here. Broken up and been in love. Traveled around the city. Saw some beautiful places. It’s funny that I’ve been to a few weekend getaways around Bangalore. And I remember even fewer. But the ones I do remember will remain in my memory for a long time. 
There are many other places I want to see in India. I want to see a desert. I want to see tigers in a national park. I want to see the ocean in Andaman. And I want to see the legendary beauty and subsequent tragedy of Jammu and Kashmir. I think I will do all of this if I live a few years more. But now I want to see a new country. Something totally different from ours. I hear stories from my friends all the time. Their adventures in South East Asia, and their travels in Europe. Vacations in US and the food in all the countries they see. 
I’ll be honest I get jealous sometimes. I want to experience all those things. I want to live in a foreign land and talk to strange people. I want to see the things these places are famous for. And all those things that I find on my own. I might. Someday. It’s difficult to say. Someday is a difficult idea. The scary thing about someday is that it might never come.
