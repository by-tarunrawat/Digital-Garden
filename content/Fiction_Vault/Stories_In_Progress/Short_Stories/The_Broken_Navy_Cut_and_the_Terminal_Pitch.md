From a line:

Most things will never happen; This one will. Vivek thought to himself. He had been trying to quit for a long time now. He has been trying for over four years now. He stopped at the signal,waiting for the cars to pass. His hands tingled as he wanted another one. He checked his pockets even though he knew he hadn’t bought a pack yesterday.
There is a shop at the next corner. Maybe I’ll get one, he thought. No. Not this shit again. How many times will I do this to myself. One becomes two which becomes a pack. No I won’t. 
The signal had the red light on. He crossed the road. Walking determined. Thought about the meeting he had later that day with his manager. “I want to quit. No that’s a little rude. I have to quit. I need to take a break. No None of these work. How can I tell him that I…” He noticed the cigarette shop from the corner of his eye as he was thinking about the meeting. 
Maybe I’ll buy one for later, he thought. I’ll need it if the meeting doesn’t go too well. But you decided… It’s play. Just one more. I can’t quit cold turkey, I need to phase it out.
“Bhaiya ek Navy cut aur ek chutki dena.” He asked the shopkeeper, who duly obliged. He paid him. Emptied the packet of chutki in his mouth and was about to light the cigarette. You were buying this for later weren’t you? If the meeting didn’t go well. He stopped for a moment. You will never be able to follow through with anything in life… Loser. He put the lighter back on the counter, broke the cigarette in two and threw I on the ground. 
Smiling he looked at the shopkeeper, who had a surprised look on his face. “Nahi wo, mai chodne ki soch raha hun.” He explained. The shopkeeper didn’t reply. He continued washing paan leaves as Vivek walked away. 
The shopkeeper sorted the bigger paan leaves from the smaller ones and arranged them on his plate. Vivek hurried back and picked up the broken cigarette. The shopkeeper looked at him. “Wo, mai dustbin me fek dunga.” HE seemed apologetic, “Chutki ke packet ke saath.” flashing the empty chutki packet and rushed off before he could hear the shopkeeper tell him there was a dustbin right there. He did see Vivek throwing something in the dustbin on the street at a distance.

***** 
Vivek rubbed his thumbs in his hands. He sweat from the armpits, even though the ac was set to 20 degrees. He scrolled down the excel sheet mindlessly, not reading anything from it. “Vivek… Vivek…” shouted the man sitting at the top of the table in the conference room. “Ya… yes sir?” Vivek replied hurriedly. “What about the MTI Bank pitch. Are we ready for tomorrow?” The man asked. “Let me check.” Vivek replied, now sitting straight going through his mail. “Kahan gayi?” He mumbled to himself.
“The pitch is tomorrow Vivek, You should be on top of these things.” THe man roared. 
“Yes… Yes sir. I am… Just…. Give me a minute” Vivek replied. THere were 6 more people in the room all looking at Vivek. He tried not to look at them. His ears turned hot. The patch of sweat around his armpit grew.
The man let out a sigh. “Don’t worry about it now, just send the pitch deck to me latest by 1”
“Sure sir.” He said, “It’s done. I’ll send it to you before that...” He said nervously, “…I think.”
The man relaxed in his chair and told everyone to get back to work. 
Everyone collected their stuff and were moving out. This is my chance to talk to him, he thought. He closed his laptop and waited for everyone to clear the room. He saw his manager collecting his stuff as well. I need to say something right now. “Vivek, stay back. I need to talk to you about something.” The manager said. 
Oh no! What now? 
The room cleared. The manger asked him to sit on the chair closest to him. Vivek nervously walked towards his boss. Million things running through his mind. What does he want to talk about? How will I tell him. Should I tell him before he says anything. No. I should wait till he completes.
“Vivek. Have their been issues at home?” the manager asked.
“No. No sir.” He replied, surprised at the question. “Why do you ask?”
“I don’t like to ask these question. Your personal life is your own. But… considering…” manger paused for a brief second. “Are you sure, everything is fine. Health wise?”
“Yeah. Everything is good.” Vivek clutched his thigh.
“The reason I’m asking all this is because, Romila noticed… She told me to… Listen… YOu seem distracted lately. Your work has suffered and…”
“Sir the pitch is ready. I just couldn’t find the…”
“Its not just the pitch Vivek. You rarely say anything in the meetings. Your replies to important mails have been… unsatisfactory. And…” manager stopped before completing his sentence, He thought carefully about the next thing he was going to say, “Listen Vivek. I want to give you the benefit of the doubt, but I have to answer to Romila. If one person in my team is not working…”
“Are you firing me?” Vivek asked, surprised. 
“Listen Vivek. I’ll help you look for another Job. I’ve made a lot of contacts in the industry, and don’t worry about references. We’ll do or best to…”
“But sir, I got two of the biggest accounts this agency…”
“That was last year Vivek. You haven’t brought in any new business since then.”
“What about StyleNinja?”
“Okay, so you got one account, but that was 15 day campaign. It doesn’t even pay the a months salary for even one designer.”
“I’ll do better Satish. Just give me a chance.” 
“I can’t do anything Vivek. I’ve negotiated a good severance…”
“I have cancer.” Vivek interrupted him. Sounding confident for the first time. 
“What?” 
“I have cancer. Lung cancer. I haven’t told anybody yet.”
Satish stared at Vivek for a long time. He realized it had been a long time since he said anything. “I.. I… I don’t know what to say Vivek. How are you doing?”
“I’ve been going for treatments.”
“Sure… Sure… I’m sorry Vivek… I… I feel a little embarrassed. Forget about what I said before. Let me talk to Romila once. Let me see what I can do.”
“Thanks sir.” Vivek replied. “I don’t like to talk about it… So I didn’t tell anyone…”
“I completely understand Vivek. Don’t worry about it.”
Complete silence for a minute. Neither says anything. “Is there anything else Satish?” Vivek finally broke the silence. “I need to finish the MTI pitch deck.”
Satish looks at Vivek angrily but his expressions turn to sympathy, “Sure sure. Just send it to me by the end of the day.”
Vivek grabs his laptop. “And Vivek. Mail me your medical reports as well. We will need it for our records.”
Vivek hesitates for a second. “Sure sir”

There are a few people in groups standing next to a tapri. Vivek walks towards it. “Bhaiya ek chai dena.”
“Aj cigarette nahi loge bhaiya.” The shopkeeper asked.
“Nahi bas chai dedo.”
He took the chai and kept it on the platform next to the tapri. He takes out the broken cigarette from his pocket. Holds the two pieces together and lights it, holding the cigarette at the crack. 
“Aur Vivek, abhi bhi yahin? Tu to quit kar raha tha na?” A tall man approached Vivek. 
“Kar diya tha yaar. Lekin kaam ka stress hi itna hai ki chodi nahi jaati.” He takes a drag.
“Mai job ki baat kar raha tha. Tu last time bol aha tha na ki tu quit karega.”
“Haan yaar. Soch to raha hun bahut dino se. Lekin kaam hi itna hai. Karega kaun?”
The tall man looks at him and realizes the answer a couple of seconds later. “Tu.”
Vivek tries to light the bottom part of the cigarette from the top part which is almost over by now. “Haan yaar. Chaar acounts dekh raha hun abhi mai. If I quit now, company bahut mushkil me aa jayegi yaar.”
“Acha… Sher hai bhai tu. Acha chal ek sutta pila.” Vivek takes him to the tapri to buy a cigarette for him.

Vivek is standing in the balcony with his mobile in his hands. He is looking at the the name Dr Bisht. He dials it.
“Hello” says Vivek.
“Hello Vivek. Thank god you returned my call. I’ve been trying to get in touch for…”
“Doctor I need a favor from you.”
“Sure Vivek. But you need to come for your treatment.”
“I will doctor. As soon as I get free from work”
“You should not be working Vivek. Any kind of stress is bad for you.”
“I know doctor. I have quit my job. But I will have to serve a month’s notice period.”
“Chalo. That’s good. But how can your company make you serve a month’s notice. Don’t they know your condition.”
“That’s why I called you doctor. I need my medical reports. I need to submit them to my company.”
“Ah. Sure sure Vivek. When will you come to collect…”
“I’m not free doctor. Lot’s of work. I told you.” Vivek bites on his lips. “Can you mail them to me.”
“Vivek I was hoping you will come in for a checkup at least.”
“I will doctor. But first can you send me my reports?”
“Haan sure. Text me your email address.”
“Will do… And doctor…” Vivek hesitates for a second. “Can you… not mention that…” Vivek bites on his lip again. “ Can you not mention that it is terminal.” There is silence on the other side of the phone, “Doctor?”
“We can still do something Vivek. You don’t need to give up…”
“Can you do it doctor?”
“We can start the chemotherapy as soon as you come in. And who knows…”
“Doctor. I don’t have much time.” Vivek contorts his face for poor choice of words. A smile comes across his face, “I mean I need to get back to…”
“I got it Vivek. I’ll send your reports.”
“Thanks doc…” Vivek slaps his forehead. Trying to be cool was never his strong suite, “..tor”
