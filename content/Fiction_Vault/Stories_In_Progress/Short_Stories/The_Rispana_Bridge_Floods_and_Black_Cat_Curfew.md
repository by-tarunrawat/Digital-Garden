Date: 21-12-2018
Topic: Write about a river

There is a river a few hundred meters in front of my house. It’s a known landmark across the city. The bridge that connects my house to the rest of the city is on the river. Rispana bridge over the Rispana river. It is a strange name for a river though. I’ve never noticed it. Rispana. Ris-pana. Risp-ana. What would it mean. I don’t know a word that would mean anything.
The river is dry. At least most times of the year. During monsoon it fills up. About 24-25 years ago, there was a flood in the river as well. The area was not as populated as it is today. There are many more concrete houses and buildings. In those days there were a few tin huts. Maybe a few bricks to support the tin roof. Most of these houses did not survive the gushing waters of the river. 
Most of the houses were on the river banks. Maybe a few yards away from the river. The ground on which the houses were built were the kind that could be damaged easily. Our house was built at a safe distance. We could see from our terrace, what the river was doing to the banks. Eroding the entire area quickly with a bull’s rage. We saw the river take people with it. Houses melted in it, taken away to some other place. 
Since then, the river banks have been rebuilt. Made up of hard concrete with a strong base. People have built their houses away from the river. I don’t think we will see a similar incident again. 
Rispana bridge has gone through its ups and downs. It is a witness to millions of stories. We have been witnesses together in a few of them. I’ve been a part of its stories since it was a small bridge that could take only a few vehicles across. Then it broke one day. It remained broken for months, maybe a couple of years. Finally rebuilt — made wider, stronger. Now it looks well dressed ready to go to work. 
There was a time a couple of decades ago, when Dehradun was in a curfew. On and off for about 3 months. It happened after a peaceful march towards Delhi, demanding a new state, was shot at by the police in a place called Rampur in Mujjafarnagar.  The incident was named ‘Rampur tiraha kaand’. Subsequently there was a curfew across the city. Anything that required going out on the road was illegal. Rispana bridge was the one of the main ways get in and out of the city. The state government posted ‘Black cat commandos’ on the bridge. They would catch trying to pass the bridge. People going to buy food, people going to hospitals. They would either beat the person caught taking the stroll or they would beat them and put them in jail for a couple of nights. I witnessed many of these incidents from my terrace. It was entertaining in a way. I was a small kid. I didn’t really know what it all really meant. I was happy with not going to school for 3 months.
