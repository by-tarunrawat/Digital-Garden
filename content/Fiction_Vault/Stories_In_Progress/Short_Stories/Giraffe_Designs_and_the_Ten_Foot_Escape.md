On its best days, Giraffe designs llc was an afterthought for local businesses in Bangalore, on its worst it was their first choice. Giraffe was a pretentious company that believed in its own ideas. The name Girrafe was a personal insight from Mr Achyutan “...it signifies the ability to look over barriers.” he used to tell clients. He was proud of that analogy. He thought it perfectly described what he wanted his company to do. Look over barriers.

Sharmita peaked from her cubicle. She wanted to see if Mr Achyutan was still there. He was. She sat back down, clicked a minimised window and started going through her facebook feed. She stopped at a picture.
Taking a break from work! Exploring life!!!!  #awayfromreallife #chillscenes #wanderlust
She stopped reading after wanderlust. She hated that word. It seemed everybody had this lust to wander and they were ready to flaunt it.
She peaked again. Mr Achyutan was on a call. “He might not notice” she thought. She walked towards the photocopier pretending she had to pick up papers. The copy machine was next to Mr Achyutan’s cabin. She could walk to the copy machine and sneak past the cabin if she was careful.

She picked up a couple of pages from the table on which the machine whirred and whizzed all day long.

Mahishasur Mantri Masala marketing pitch

STRATEGIES TO INCREASE PAGEVIEWS
• Search engine optimization
• Paid ads
• Social media marketing
• Viral content...

“Same old shit!” she looked inside the cabin.
“Oh shit! Did he just see me?” she asked herself. “Should I go back to my cubicle?”. She was unsure. Mr Achyutan was still on a call and he might have glanced in her direction. “Doesn’t matter. I was anyway trying to get a few papers from the copy machine” She told herself. “Even if he saw me, I was doing my job.”

She was now facing her cubicle. She took a step. Stopped. Looked inside the cabin again. Mr Achyutan’s back was towards her. “This is it! He won’t see me go out.”

Her next stop. Toilet. She tip-toed through the gallery. A pungent smell invaded her nose like invisible little Roman soldiers, destroying everything in its path and settling inside the fort to takeover her brain. “Eww” she was convinced her head vomited a little. She had spent 2 years 5 months and 21 days out of her 2 years 5 months and 23 days in the company avoiding coming here. Officially it was for all employees, but the blame for its, literal, decay were the males of the species. She wanted to get out of there as soon as possible, but this was the only place in the office not visible from the cabin.

She looked at the main door. It was 15ft from the toilet. She calculated that it would take her 15-20 seconds to walk out; if she moved fast, she could cover the distance in 5 seconds. However, the office was unusually quite, and running would surely bring notice to her. Quickly she made a decision to walk out on her toes without making any noise.

Her heart started racing. She could feel a thousand wild buffaloes running in her chest. Tiny beads of perspiration formed on her forehead. She stopped hearing sounds. Her ears were hot.  
She took her first step then another. “Another 10 feet.” she thought “Almost there.”
“AAACCHHHHUUU”

Sharmita froze.

A couple of months ago somebody sneezed in the office and Mr Achyutan, came running out of his cabin to greet what he thought was his mother coming for a surprise visit.
Anyway, since that day, it became a running joke.
Sneeze. Wait for the lecture. Giggle.

Sharmita realized she had been standing on her toes for longer than what was required from ballet dancers on a good days work.
“Stupid. Stttuuupid. Stuuuupppiidd” she thought “What should I do now? Did he see me? Should I look to check?”
Her thoughts were louder than usual. She had to speak up over the sound of her heart beating.
“Move at least... Any direction... Don’t just stand there.”

This was defeat. She held the papers in both hands. Turned, wiped the perspiration on her forehead, and walked back.

“Mr Achyutan.” she said as he came out of his office, phone in his left hand, his right hand trying to block the obscenities, he was about to shout, reaching the other side.
“I think I took your copies by mistake. I thought there were mine.”

He looked at her, signalled her to follow him to the cabin and started talking on the phone.
“Nothing. Office kids. Mmhmm, mmhhmm. Yeah, no probelm. I’ll be there in half hour.”

“Ms Singhal. What do you want?” he turned towards Sharmita.
“Just these papers. I picked them up from the copy machine thinking they were mine. I was thinking of going through the Digitech presentation I have tomorrow.” said Sharmita.
“Stop explaining so much. It sounds like you’re lying. SHUT UP and hand him the papers” she thought.

“hmmm. Come back to my office later. I’ll review the Digitech presentation. We don’t want any mistakes. Do we?” said Mr Achyutan. He wasn’t expecting an answer from Sharmita. She nodded her head anyway.
“Take these pages with you. I’ve jotted down some points for the Mahishasur Pitch. Research the company. Go to the market to check out competition. Conduct a few interviews with customers.” He paused for a moment to look for his keys. “The pitch is Monday next week. I want this done by Friday so that I can review it.”
Sharmita’s grip on the pages tightened. Her ears were getting hot again. She struggled to stop her thoughts from coming out of her mouth. She knew what was coming.
“We don’t want any mistakes. Do we?” said Mr Achyutan as he walked out.

She looked at the pages again. “I can’t use anything from here”. She figured she will have to do everything. In 3 days.

Sharmita stood there for a few seconds. Turned around, threw the pages in the dustbin and walked out of the cabin. Crossing the toilet through the passage she finally breaching the main door.

........

“How much?” Sharmita asked as she handed the shopkeeper 13 Rs for the cigarette. He took the money and nodded. This had become a routine for her. Everyday, once before lunch and once before evening tea, she would go down the building, walk up the road to the small shop.

She thought about how routine her life had become. Everyday she would get up at 8:30 after snoozing the alarm twice. She had to reach to the office by 9.30. Fumbling through her morning routine of ultra mild, shower, tea and bowel movements, she would always manage to leave by 9, only to get late because the uber driver couldn’t find the location, autorickshaws suddenly realized they deserved double the amount they normally charged, or Bangalore traffic.

Her thoughts were broken by the vibration in her pants. She did not want to talk to anyone right now. She looked at the phone. It was Pradeep. Hesitatingly she picked up.
“Hello”
“Out for a smoke break. Hmmm. No, I’ll eat in an hour.”
She took a couple of drags “ummm....Pradeep...” she paused. An argument is the last thing she wanted. “... is it okay, if I am not able to come tonight” She was hurrying through her cigarette now.
“I have a lot of work” she did not want to deal with him tonight. “I don’t know. I have a couple of pitches to work on.”
“Not tonight. Please. Please. Please.” she screamed inside
“uh-huh... uh-huh... But I might get late. you’ll have to wait a while.” her voice had softened.
“uh-huh...okay, If you want to. There’ll be a spare key with the guard.”
“cool. Bye.”

She disconnected the phone and looked at the screen.

7 unread messages from dad. She was avoiding talking to both of them today. She knew what they wanted from her was not something she was ready for.

She looked at the cigarette. Ash hanging. Tobacco all but smoked.
