Topic: “I would like to make an exchange”

“What do you want?”, he was repairing a shoe. Piercing the needle through the sole, he hung a string at the top of the needle on a hook, and pulled it. 
“I would like to make an exchange.” I said. This was the first time I had spoken to him. He continued working on the shoe. I cleared my throat and repeated myself, “I would like to make an exchange.”
“I heard you,” he said without looking up, “I didn’t think you were finished.”
He was calm. Working on the shoe. He was good at that. The stitches around the shoe were neat. “I want to exchange my life. I don’t want to live this one anymore.”
For the first time since I met him, he stopped. He looked up at me for a few seconds. He looked at me from up to down and his gaze settled on my feet. “You need to fix those shoes,” he said, “I can fix them up for you if you want.” He looked up at me. His eyes were black with tiny spots of blue and white in them. I couldn’t help but stare at them. 
“It’s fine. I’ll buy new ones.” I jerked myself from the trance. He went back to stitching the shoe in his hand. “I want to exchange my life. I was told you could help me with that,” I said.
“Who told you?” 
“I don’t remember.”
“You don’t remember?”
“I don’t know.” I honestly had no recollection of the person who sent me here. I remember a voice. It was a man. He told me to meet ‘the cobbler’.
“How did you come here?” He dipped a brush in the box of black wax and started polishing the shoe. 
“I came by…” How did I come here? The voice had told me to meet ‘the cobbler’, it had not given me any directions. “I don’t know how I came here.” I looked around. We were on top of a mountain. The sun was setting. It had been setting since I got here. I don’t think it moved. The whole mountain was barren. There was some grass, that a cow was nibbling on. ‘The cobbler’ was sitting with his back on the edge of the hill. 
“What is this place?” I asked. He brought the shoe close to his face and pulled up his nose from the tip. 
“This is really good isn’t it?” He hung his hand out with the shoe in it for me to see.
“Yes. It’s great. Now about…”
“Come closer,” He said. He had a smile on his face. I was standing a meter from him. I took a step forward and bent down. It really was a good job. The shoe was shining. I could see my face on it. I looked at him. He was still smiling. A smile appeared on my lips. He nodded and kept the shoe on the ground.
“Where do you think you are?” He asked.
“Am I dreaming?” I asked back.
“Do your dreams look like this?” He asked again.
I was irritated. He was answering my questions with questions of his own. “I don’t know. I don’t care. Can’t you just tell me where I am?”
“I can. But you wouldn’t believe me.” He said.
“Try me.”
“You are in your head.” He said.
What does he mean? Is he saying this is all in my imagination? Then why does it feel so real? “Why does it feel so real?” I asked him.
“Have you ever seen a rose?” He asked another question. I was getting fed up with his questions. I didn’t want to be rude to him. I had come to him for help.
“No. I have never seen a rose in my life.” I said. He gave out a chuckle.
“How do you know that the roses you’ve ‘never’ seen are real?” 
“I know because… I can touch it, I can see its colors, its form its shape. I can smell its fragrance.”
“But how do you know what you touch, smell and see is the rose.”
“Well, I see it with my eyes, which sends the signal back to my brain and my brain figures out what it is.”
“So your brain tells you it’s real?”
“Yes.” I looked at him anticipating another question, but he just stared at me. 
He was right. My brain makes everything real. So I was imagining this. But I wanted to exchange my life. If this is not real, then that is not going to happen. I should just leave this place. If I am in my imagination, then I can leave anytime I want. Can’t I?
“No,” the cobbler said. 
“What?” I asked.
“You can’t leave anytime you want.”
How did he know that? Can he read my mind? 
“I am in your mind. I am a part of it. If you think it I know it.”
“So I can’t leave. I can’t exchange my life…”
“You can exchange your life.” The cobbler spoke again, “and you can leave here as well.”
