Date: 16-01-2019
Topic: Someone’s playing the radio

It has the crackling of static. There is some hymn playing on the radio. It sounds like a prayer. Must be some muslim family getting ready for evening prayers. It’s loud. It seems like it’s far away. I can hear it but the sound diminishes as I walk out of the room. 
Its an old radio. They’ve taken good care of it. It’s chipped at a corner. But polished and cleaned. This would have been in their family for a long time. Or might have been passed down from some friend. I’m making assumptions of course. It is entirely possible they bought it second hand from some shop. 
I would imagine them to have phones though. But not everyone will have access to it. Imagine a household without a TV, that is struggling to keep ends meet. Such a family would not be able to afford mobiles for everyone in the family. They cant afford to waste money on TV and cable bills. They would need a cheaper mode of entertainment. I can imagine them buying a radio for a few hundred bucks.
So if they are playing a channel that broadcasts hymns and prayers, then they would do it pretty regularly. They would do it about 5 times a day. Rest of the time they can play any other entertainment or music channel. There probably would be fights about what gets played. The father probably doesn’t care, except during namaz. THe mother holds the power, and the kids get to negotiate. 
Children don’t listen to the radio that much anyway. They go to their friend’s place to watch TV. Even the mom probably goes to the neighbors place to watch her TV shows. They only listen to it at night. When they are together at home. The father is an orthodox, strict parent. But he is a good man. He works his cart of vegetables everyday, brings the money home, saves for his kids’ education and future. The mom works as a maid. She is a smart, sharp woman. She is extremely observant and action oriented. She keeps the household together. She manages the finances. She earns more than her husband, so she always has money to spend on things for her family. They got married quite young. Mom’s father was a tailor. Dad’s father was a farmer. Their dad’s knew each other from their native village. So, the granddads got together and fixed the marriage. They were both 20.
Granddad (Mom’s father) had this radio that he used to listen to every morning and evening with his tea. He raised mom alone. His wife had been killed in riots. He had a lot of anger in his heart. He wanted to kill the people responsible for his wife’s death. He was okay with dying for the cause. In fact, he was hoping for it. He had a choice between his daughter and revenge. He chose his daughter. He never forgave the people responsible for it. He doesn’t really know their identity. SO his rage is directed at all Hindus. The radio was the last present his wife had given him. He used to drink his morning tea with his wife when she was alive. Now he does it with the radio.
The radio was finally passed down to Mom after her father died. He didn’t have much to pass down to her. He had already given her mom’s jewelery to her for her wedding. Some of which she had sold to manage finances in her house. He had a radio and his shop. Mom and dad had sold the shop to buy the house they were living in. They spent some of the money to buy dad a tempo to sell the vegetables from. He could drive around instead of pushing the cart in the heat.
