## Act I: The Inciting Dilemma & The Juhi Dialogue

The dilemma at the heart of the story is the battle between what the world expects and what you want to become—especially when you don’t yet know who you are.

She thinks her problem is her job, her relationship, and the relentless pressure from her father. The surface dilemma: stick it out, say yes to the boyfriend and father, and live an expected, safe life—or just run away to make the noise stop.

### Scene: Coffee with Juhi
> **HER:** What if I just go somewhere?  
> **JUHI:** Go somewhere? You want to go on a trip?  
> **HER:** No. Just go. Quit my job, and just run away without telling anyone.  
> **JUHI:** *(Laughs)* Sure. Where will you go?  
> **HER:** I don't know. The hills. Maybe Himachal.  
> **JUHI:** You know I have a friend... She lives in Shimla. She likes it there. But she has to walk all the way to the store to get groceries. Amazon delivers in 10 days. You don't even get Uber there. What will you do?  
> **HER:** I'll write. Travel. I don't know.  
> **JUHI:** Why do you want to go? You have a good job. A steady relationship. It's more than what most have.  
> **HER:** Leave it.  
> **JUHI:** Tell me.  
> **HER:** No, leave it.  
> **JUHI:** Arey tell me yaar.  
> **HER:** I hate my job. I hate it I hate it I hate it.  
> **JUHI:** Change it. Take another job. It's fine.  
> **HER:** It'll be the same. I don't feel like...  
> **JUHI:** Feel like what?  
> **HER:** I don't know how to explain it. I hate working in an office. I hate the work I'm doing, I hate answering to my—  
> **JUHI:** DO something you like then.  
> **HER:** What?  
> **JUHI:** Do something you like! You hate the work here, right? So just think: what do you want to do, and do it.  
> **HER:** Mmmm-hmmm.  
> **JUHI:** Like, what do you like doing?  
> **HER:** Many things man. Like... playing badminton.  
> **JUHI:** Okay! Write a badminton blog. Coach young kids.  
> **HER:** What are you talking about?  
> **JUHI:** Yeah. Do what you like!  
> **HER:** Do you hear yourself? How idiotic you sound. Just start coaching? What do I even know about coaching? I like playing badminton, I don't want to do it all my life. What the fuck, Juhi.  
> **JUHI:** I was just...  
> **HER:** So. Just run away.

## Act II: The Boyfriend Arc (Love vs. Moving On)

### Thematic Focus: Defining Love
She initially thinks the relationship is tying her down because she is stuck in her own skin. She assumed love meant "completing" each other, but that makes romance purely transactional. 

True love is enhancing: recognizing someone deeply, understanding their flaws and strengths, accepting them, and supporting them without keeping a ledger.

### The Climax Beat
She returns after months in the mountains, finally grounded in herself. She expects a dramatic reckoning where she either asks for him back or delivers a noble, tearful goodbye.

Instead, when she meets him, he listens to her apology calmly and reveals that he has simply moved on. He has been dating someone else for two months. 

The realization stings, but there is no breakdown. She realizes she had a chance at love, lost it through her own avoidance, but is fundamentally intact. She doesn't need him to rescue her; she can take care of herself.

## Act III: The Boss Confrontation (Airport Lounge)

### Scene: Running into Sanjay
En route to Delhi, killing time in the airport lounge with free food and a book, she sits down directly across from Sanjay, her former boss.

> **SANJAY:** Hello? What happened to you? Where were you? You just vanished.  
> **HER:** Hi. I was doing some soul searching.  
> **SANJAY:** *(Laughs)* Whose soul were you searching? Anyway, how've you been? Where are you working now?  
> **HER:** Nowhere.  
> **SANJAY:** Really? I thought you left to join a competitor.  
> **HER:** No. I ran away. Spent time in Himachal and Uttarakhand. Now I'm writing.  
> **SANJAY:** Writing. That's... great.  
> **HER:** Sanjay, tell me something. When you told me we’d do big things next year—that I’d get the promotion and the pay raise—did you actually mean it? Or was it just the standard script to keep me billing forty extra hours a week?  
> **SANJAY:** Of course I meant it. We have a business to run—  
> **HER:** I know you have a business to run. But could you try being honest with people? Just once. Without the corporate lecture.

She walks to her gate without anger. He was never an evil mastermind—just another small-time manipulator exploiting convenience.

## Act IV: The Father Resolution (Revoking the Approval Trap)

### Thematic Focus: Breaking Conditional Control
Her prolonged inaction in life was not her father's fault; it was her own cowardice in seeking his permission. She used his authoritarian expectations (arranged marriage pressure, corporate career tracks) as an excuse to avoid taking responsibility for her own choices.

### The Resolution Beat
She confronts him not with fury or adolescent rebellion, but with grounded gratitude and a clean boundary:

> *"I love you, and I will always remember how you took care of me—taking me to school, helping me with homework, traveling together. I will always want your advice. But you have to let go.
> 
> My life belongs to me now. If I end up broke, disgraced, or miserable, they have to be my mistakes. Because even if I do everything you say, you cannot guarantee my success or my happiness. Only I have to live with the outcome."*

She leaves him without needing an apology or an endorsement.

## Act 0.5: Roshan's Silence & The Fake NGO Applications

### The Withheld "I Love You"
The friction with Roshan (the boyfriend) isn't loud or explosive; it's a cold, prolonged starvation. 
In two full years of dating, she has never said *"I love you."* 
She assumed her silence was a gentle buffer, signaling the relationship's natural limits. Instead, he mistook her emotional distance for modest reserve, using it to rationalize stepping past her to ask her father for permission to marry.

### The Application Cover
To justify her impending disappearance, she fabricates experience on international NGO fellowship applications in public development—listing volunteer work for an uncle's trust. The application advice says *"be true to yourself,"* and she cynically calculates: *"I've put in enough palatable bullshit to make them think I'm exactly who they want."*

## Subplot Option: The Bumbling Extortion Scheme (The Boss & The Colleague)

In a darker, satirical variant of the narrative arc:
When she vanishes from the office without submitting a formal resignation, her manager (facing payroll shortfalls) and a desperate subordinate uncover where she went through a mutual friend. 

Rather than reporting her absence responsibly, they concoct a half-baked scheme to call her authoritarian father and demand ransom, pretending she has been abducted before she reaches Himachal. The plan collapses immediately when her father contacts the police, leading to their arrest in an absurd, botched payoff.

## Act 0.2: The Promotion Trap (Ramesh and the Creative Director Bait)

### Scene: Ramesh's Office
Somya attempts to claim the career trajectory promised during her recruitment two years ago.

> **SOMYA:** Hi Ramesh, can I talk to you for a moment?  
> **RAMESH:** Sure, I have five minutes.  
> **SOMYA:** So... I wanted to talk to you about my role. You know I'm an advertising major, and I came to this agency because I was promised a creative position if I did good work.  
> *(Ramesh stares at her in silence.)*  
> **SOMYA:** Since you said you were happy with my work, I was hoping I could interview for the open Creative Director position.  
> **RAMESH:** That's a good idea. Ganesh already has a lot of workload and could use help. Are you ready for it? Good is fine, Somya. I need great. Do you commit to being great?  
> **SOMYA:** Yes, sir. I will be.  
> **RAMESH:** Most important thing is never to disappoint the client. So what we'll do is: talk to Ganesh, let him offload some of his grunt work to you. We'll check your progress on a schedule and see if you can handle the responsibility.  
> **SOMYA:** And... the title?  
> **RAMESH:** You have to complete your existing assignments on time as well. I'm not going to hand out a position without testing you. Would you do that if you ran this company?  
> **SOMYA:** No...  
> **RAMESH:** Exactly. Talk to Ganesh, finish your existing load, and we'll review your progress.  
> **SOMYA:** In how much time, sir?  
> **RAMESH:** About a year. That sounds fair. If you can't take the responsibility, it's fine. We can have this conversation again when you're ready.  
> **SOMYA:** No... sir. I'm ready.  
> **RAMESH:** Very well then.

## Protagonist Interiority: Somya's Dual Deceptions

### The Secret She Won't Tell Herself
She convinces herself that escaping to Himachal or landing an overseas fellowship will solve her life[cite: 68]. In reality, the fellowship is an academic hiding spot—a two-year shield to dodge a breakup and freeze her father's marriage demands without having to confront either[cite: 68]. Her partner is attentive and loyal; her guilt stems from the fact that she simply doesn't love him, yet lacks the courage to hurt him[cite: 68].

### The Secret She Won't Tell Anyone Else
At 32, societal panic is closing in[cite: 69]. She dodges arranged marriage biodatas from her father, but secretly fears that walking away from a stable relationship leaves her unmarriageable in the eyes of her family and peers[cite: 69]. She stays silent not out of contentment, but out of fear that she lacks the skills to survive outside prescribed corporate tracks[cite: 69].
