Date: 17-11-2018
Topic: Write about a ceremony

I’ve attended a few hundred ceremonies in my life. The visuals that come to mind thinking about ceremonies are ‘Mallu weddings’ and ‘Haldi haath’ in a traditional north Indian wedding. 
I attended my first mallu wedding a couple of years ago, in the southernmost part of India — Kanyakumari. It was my friend Vivek’s wedding. The best thing I like about these weddings is that they are short. Extremely short. 5-10 minute tops. Rest of it is just photographs and food and all. Someone who was warning me about the duration of mallu weddings explained it like this: Once I went to a wedding. Parking in the hotel the ceremony was in was full. I tried finding space for my car somewhere. It didn’t take much time. But as I reached the wedding hall. It was over. The bride and groom were getting their pictures clicked with the guests. Anyway, I love this about it. You don’t have to sit there listening to the pandit chant boring mantras that you don’t understand anyway. Everybody facing the stage as if there is a show going on. There is a show going on infact. But its a boring show. You would never pay money to watch such a boring show. You wouldn’t generally go for such a show if it was free. 
Another thing that I love about these weddings is the food. It’s not that the food is exceptionally good. It is good no doubt. But what I like about it is its simplicity and uniformness across weddings. People never go for any fancy cuisine. It is the same food everywhere. Sambhar-rice, rasam, a couple of veggies, chutneys, a simple water drink and that’s all. Unlike north Indian weddings, guests can’t say this or that wasn’t there. Guests are full of complaints at north Indian weddings. In mallu weddings, since the food is all same, there can be no complaints. You get what you expect. 
Another, visual I get thinking about ceremonies is, haldi haath. It’s weird. It is done for both girls and boys. They are dovered in haldi first. All the relatives come and put haldi on their bodies. This is done till all the relatives have done it. Bride and groom are covered fully with haldi at the end of it. Completely yellow. Worst case of jaundice you’ll ever see. Then they are given a bath in front of everyone. Normally you woudn’t want to see anyone bathe. You wouldn’t let anyone see you bathe. But this is considered fine. I don’t understand why things you wouldn’t do in normal life are considered okay during a wedding. Maybe it’s not weird. Maybe I’m just being cynical. Haldi is considered to be good for your skin. It also has some health benefits I think. That is the reason given for this ceremony anyway. It’s just weird to me. How can you let strangers touch you, then bathe you, and watch you get wet in white clothes. It’s amazing what happens.
