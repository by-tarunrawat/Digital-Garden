Topic: Find your way in a city

I needed to find new places to eat and drink. The first time I came to Bangalore, I was dependent on a friend to show me around. Even after living there for a couple of months, I didn’t know anymore than the area I was living in. I knew a few places in Sanjay Nagar. I didn’t know how to find places though. I could never differentiate between the 80 ft road, and the new Bel road. I still can’t. Thankfully we have Google maps to show us the way. Only if it could show us the way in life. It would be our god.

The best thing I did after I moved in the apartment, which was in Indiranagar, was to go everywhere on foot. Any distance close to two, two and a half kilometers, I would walk. It was after that that you could tell me an address and I would find the approximate location without google. It was a blessing that I was living in a good location. Anything worth going was located within that two and a half km range. 
Initially I traveled longer distances in cabs. So I had an idea how to reach certain places. I’m not certain though. I don’t think I can travel within Bangalore without a map, if I was driving on my own. If I had a bike or something, and I was riding it around bangalore, I would familiarize myself with the city in about a month. 
I remember when I was in Ahmedabad for two years, we would travel around in auto rickshaws. I had no clue of where different places were. When I actually started living there on my own, I got familiar with the city in a month. Maybe less. I had my bike there, and although, my office was pretty close, I would still ride to different places alone. I tried to look for new places to eat and go there on my bike. The city started making sense to me. I finally understood the directions to places I used to go earlier. 
There is this strange thing that happens when you finally get to know a city. There were malls and multiplexes and restaurants that I used to go to earlier. But I had no clue where they were in the city. But after going to these places on my own, made it familiar in some way. I could call it my city now. 
It is similar to what happens when you start a relationship with someone. Initially you don’t know much about them. You see them with awe, you are apprehensive, you talk about general things. Then you start to know each other. You become familiar. You could say things you couldn’t before. You see them in a new light. At first they are something else to you. That guy who sings really well. That pretty girl. That girl who says all the smart things. Then all of a sudden. After hanging out with them, they become something else. They are your friends, your colleagues, girlfriends and boyfriends, classmates.
It is the same with a city. You start with that city that you know superficially. Then it is the city that has this or that. And finally it becomes that city where I…

There is a smidgen of truth to that. In the city it is just another sound. Adding to the crowd of noises that is always there — standing on the fence, making noise, but after a while ignored and made part of the background. There is no love in that. There is a longing in love. I don’t want the train to come. I don’t want to hear the loud victory march of the train driver over another human settlement. Along with the car horns and street vendors and running engines. Like an accompaniments of an orchestra that doesn’t go with each other. Out of rhythm and playing different tunes.

The train blows it’s trumpet once again and it goes beautifully with the rhythm. The silence plays a part. It provides the perfect background for this concert to work.
We don’t value silence anymore. We want to fill it up with something. There is an addiction to sound. We hear it everywhere in the background. We have built an immunity to it. When we are alone with our thoughts, we look for noise. In our phones. On the TV in the background while you’re working on something on your laptop. We think that noise gives us company. # Writer's Logic: Subtext and Tension

Dramatic dialogue is rarely about information transfer; it is an exercise in power navigation and unspoken status.

### The Cockpit Gradient in Drama
When two characters have an asymmetric power dynamic, conflict surfaces as indirect language and polite evasion. 

For the mechanics of this dynamic under pressure, see [[01_Seed/Boeing CRM|Crew Resource Management]]. When writing scenes with escalating stakes, polite deference between characters often ratchets up tension far better than open shouting.

Skills:
Story writing - Writing stories based on different story structure. Creating plot. World Building. Themes. 
Screenplay - Constructing scenes that make an impact. Bring new elements. Move plot forward. Reveal character. 
Character development - Create characters based on real world traits. Character contradictions and motivations. Developing character arcs.  
Show development - Short term (weekly stories). Long term (season arc).

Rules for episodic story:
1. Character changes only a little. 
2. Each episode has an arc. A problem at the beginning, the character struggle in the middle, and the solution at the end. 

Rules for Daily soap:
1. Create big moments that have an impact.
2. Always end on a cliffhanger to get the viewer want to watch the next episode. 
3. Viewers are not always watching, spoon-feed the story. Repeat plot points if you have to. Let characters reveal what happened before. 
4. Don't be afraid to make big pivots. Eg. Character dies, character comes back, they take a heel turn etc. 

Rules for limited story:
1. The story should have a beginning (The problem/conflict), the middle (Act 2), and the end (Climax). 
2. The conflict arises from what character wants and what are the obstacles. This is the conflict that moves the story. 
3. Characters should have an arc. At the end they should have changed (There are exceptions). 
4. At the midpoint the character should change tactics (usually).


Story structures:
3 act structure.

story circle.

The Writer's Room:

Story process: 

Broad story: For a season long show or a daily soap, we create broad beats the main character is going to hit. What are the main problems they are going to face in their journey. How will those main problems be solved at the end of the broad story. 

Episodic story: After the broad story is made, you get specific. Create a rough lineup of how the broad story is going to be divided into a chunk of episodes (let's say 10 episodes). Then that is further divided into individual episode. 
When writing a particular episode you decide what the objectives are for that episode. For eg. the objective could be to reveal a secret to the character. Now you decide how that is going to happen through different circumstances and different characters. Introduce conflict. Conflict is the fuel for drama. 

Adaptation: To adapt a novel or a story for screen, you need to understand what the story is about first. Then list down the scenes in the novel to determine what scenes are descriptive and which ones are visual. Analyze all the scenes, which ones are required and which ones can be cut. Figure out if the descriptive scenes are important to the story. If yes, then make the descriptive scenes visual. Create a broad story and a character bible.
